TWO DIFFERENT BIASES, PULLING IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS. Reading the bias
table by row rather than by column is the whole point:

  Specs A and B are not merely attenuated, they have the WRONG SIGN on
  two of three categories -- they report demand RISING with price. With no
  fixed effects, a pooled regression is identified off the price gap
  BETWEEN products, not off any product's response to its own price. It is
  comparing a $6 item to a $2 item and calling the difference elasticity.
  Adding the promo control (B) changes essentially nothing, because the
  problem was never the promo flag.

  Specs C and D add series fixed effects, which fixes the sign but leaves
  the estimate badly attenuated toward zero. That residue is a LIKELIHOOD
  problem: unit sales are counts with a large zero mass, and log1p(sales)
  is not log(sales) -- on a series that is zero most days the transform
  crushes exactly the observations carrying the price signal. No amount of
  further control fixes a misspecified link.

  Spec E (Poisson GLM, log link, but NO promo control) is biased AWAY from
  zero -- demand looks more elastic than it is. That IS the identification
  problem: prices in this panel were not randomised. The generator
  schedules promotions into weeks demand is already strong
  (src/generate.py::_price_path), and a promotion carries a display lift
  on top of its price cut. Low price and high demand therefore arrive
  together, and the regression charges the co-movement to price.

  Spec F (Poisson GLM + promo control + series FE + week-of-year) fixes
  both and lands close to truth. It is the number the markdown simulator
  uses.

The honest ceiling: spec F is still OBSERVATIONAL. It recovers truth here
because I know what confound to control for -- I wrote the generator. On
real data there is no such guarantee, and no specification search can
establish that the remaining price variation is as-good-as-random. That
takes a price experiment: randomised price cells across matched stores.
Section 9 prices exactly what not having run one costs.

WHAT THIS TABLE SAYS, in the order a merchant would ask:

1. Markdown depth is a function of elasticity, and the optimiser works
   that out without being told. Read sched_oracle down the categories:
     FOODS      true e=-2.10  ->  30/30/40   (sell-through 96% vs 45% at full price)
     HOUSEHOLD  true e=-1.20  ->  40/50/50   (sell-through 88% vs 45% at full price)
     HOBBIES    true e=-0.60  ->  0/0/0   (sell-through 45% vs 45% at full price)
   An inelastic category should not be marked down at all -- cutting price
   there gives away margin on units that were going to sell anyway. A
   markdown budget spread evenly across categories spends most of itself
   where it repays least.

2. THE COST OF THE ELASTICITY ESTIMATE, IN DOLLARS. bad_spec_cost_pct is
   the revenue given up by a team that estimated elasticity the way most
   portfolio projects do -- log-log OLS on a zero-heavy count series:
     FOODS      bad spec  28.94% of clearance revenue   |  good spec  0.43%
     HOUSEHOLD  bad spec   6.58% of clearance revenue   |  good spec  0.51%
     HOBBIES    bad spec   0.00% of clearance revenue   |  good spec  0.00%
   The attenuated estimate makes demand look inelastic, so the BAD team
   marks down too little or not at all and eats the salvage. That number
   is the business case for the price experiment, priced.

3. SENSITIVITY (elasticity +/-30% around truth):
     FOODS      good-vs-no-markdown across worlds: +13.99%, +40.12%, +45.46% -> DECISION HOLDS
     HOUSEHOLD  good-vs-no-markdown across worlds: -10.65%, +6.50%, +20.71% -> DECISION FLIPS
     HOBBIES    good-vs-no-markdown across worlds: +0.00%, +0.00%, +0.00% -> DECISION HOLDS
   Where it holds, the recommendation survives a 30% elasticity error even
   though the revenue forecast does not -- the level moves, the decision
   does not. Where it flips, the honest answer is that the category cannot
   be priced off observational data and needs a test before margin is bet.

4. good_vs_flat_pct is small throughout. That is the honest size of the
   phasing win: with a stated demand half-life, most of the value is in
   marking down AT ALL, not in the shape of the path. A markdown-
   optimisation project that headlines the phasing gain and hides the
   mark-down-or-not gain has its emphasis backwards.
