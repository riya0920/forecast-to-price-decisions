ML-1 M5 PASS -- THE SCALE CLAIM, ON REAL DATA
==============================================================================
'No real dataset. M5 is not downloadable here.' -- half false. The
competition download 403s until its rules are accepted, but the daily
sales panel is republished as a plain Kaggle DATASET.

PROVENANCE: tbierhance/m5-forecasting-parquet-and-aggregations, a
third-party re-encoding rather than the official archive, not diffed
against it because the official one is the gated one.

==============================================================================
A. IS THE GENERATOR'S DEMAND SHAPED LIKE M5'S?
==============================================================================
  M5 panel                 : 30490 series x 1941 days
  zero share               : 0.6346
  median inter-demand gap  : 2.475 days
  median CV^2 of nonzeros  : 0.474

  Two thirds of M5 is zeros. That is the property the generator was
  built to imitate and the reason WMAPE misbehaves on it -- and it is
  worth confirming on the real panel before trusting any conclusion
  drawn from a simulated one.

==============================================================================
B. DOES THE DEEP ARM CATCH UP AS THE PANEL GROWS?
==============================================================================
Same architecture, same loss, same 28-day horizon, and -- this is the
part that makes it an experiment -- THE SAME TRAINING BUDGET. Every arm
sees ~150000 windows for 8 epochs; only the number of distinct series
those windows are drawn from changes.

  Holding epochs fixed and letting the window count grow with the panel
  would confound BREADTH with VOLUME: 30,000 series at stride 3 is 18
  million windows, and an arm that wins there has been given sixty
  times the compute as well as a hundred times the series. The claim
  under test is about breadth, so breadth is what varies.

    300 series -> stride   3, 150000 windows,    76s
   3000 series -> stride  36, 150000 windows,    94s
  30000 series -> stride 366, 150000 windows,    87s

 series  stride  windows  nbeats_wmape  nbeats_bias  snaive_wmape  snaive_bias  fit_seconds
    300       3   150000        0.6514      -0.3609        0.8174      -0.0830      76.2000
   3000      36   150000        0.6748      -0.3625        0.8553      -0.0811      94.2000
  30000     366   150000        0.6712      -0.3367        0.8627      -0.0736      87.2000

  RAW WMAPE IS NOT COMPARABLE ACROSS THESE ROWS, and reading it that way
  was the first mistake this section made. Each arm is a DIFFERENT
  SAMPLE of series, and the samples are not equally hard: the seasonal
  naive scores 0.8174 on the 300-series sample and 0.8627 on the 30,000.
  A model whose absolute WMAPE holds flat across those two is doing
  BETTER on the second, not worse.

  FVA against the seasonal naive is the comparison that survives,
  because both terms are computed on the same series:

      300 series : naive 0.8174  n-beats 0.6514  FVA +0.1660
     3000 series : naive 0.8553  n-beats 0.6748  FVA +0.1805
    30000 series : naive 0.8627  n-beats 0.6712  FVA +0.1914

  THE SCALE CLAIM HOLDS, MODESTLY AND ONLY ON THE RIGHT METRIC. FVA
  rises +0.1660 -> +0.1805 -> +0.1914 as the panel widens, on a FIXED
  training budget -- so it is breadth buying the improvement rather
  than gradient steps. '300 series is far below where global deep
  models start paying' was directionally right.

  But read the size before staffing anything: a hundred times the
  series buys +0.0255 of FVA. That is a real gain and it is not the
  step change the phrase 'start paying' suggests.

  AND THE BIAS DOES NOT IMPROVE AT ALL, WHICH IS THE REAL FINDING.

      300 series : n-beats bias -0.3609   naive bias -0.0830
     3000 series : n-beats bias -0.3625   naive bias -0.0811
    30000 series : n-beats bias -0.3367   naive bias -0.0736

  On the generator this project measured N-BEATS at -20.6% bias and
  called the WMAPE gap 'the boring half' -- the real problem being that
  a model a fifth low on every order is a different and worse decision.
  On real M5 it forecasts 35% LOW, worse than on the generator, and
  a hundred times the series does not move it.

  THAT IS WHY IT BEATS THE NAIVE ON WMAPE. Mean-scaled absolute error on
  a panel that is 63% zeros REWARDS forecasting low: a low forecast is
  right on the many zero days and wrong only on the few that sell. The
  deep arm is winning the metric by exploiting it, and a replenishment
  system driven by it would under-order every slow mover it owns.

  So the honest verdict is split. The SCALE explanation was
  directionally right and worth little. The BIAS diagnosis was right
  and is worth more on real data than it was on the generator.

  WHAT IS NOT CONTROLLED: compute is held fixed on purpose, so this
  cannot say whether more EPOCHS would help; and the GBM arm is not
  rerun here, so 'N-BEATS 0.5656 against the GBM 0.5291' has no real-
  data counterpart. The baseline here is the seasonal naive.

==============================================================================
WHAT M5 CANNOT DO
==============================================================================
  No known elasticity, no planted cross-price effect, no ground-truth
  substitute set. Every scored-against-truth result in this project
  stays on the generator: real data can falsify a claim about model
  behaviour, and it cannot supply a number nobody measured.
