  Pinball loss got WORSE at 1 of 5 quantiles.

  Five independently fitted quantile models have nothing coupling them,
  so the fitted P90 can land ABOVE the fitted P95 -- and it does, on
  1.0% of item-days. That is not a numerical wart. An order system
  reading a crossed fan orders MORE stock for a LOWER service level,
  which inverts the meaning of the dial a planner is turning.

  Monotone rearrangement -- sorting the fan at each point -- is the
  Chernozhukov-Fernandez-Val-Galichon fix, and its appeal is that it is
  free: the true quantile function is monotone, so sorting an estimate
  moves it toward the truth in POPULATION.

  That guarantee is a population statement, and the table shows the
  finite-sample reality: 4 of 5 quantiles improved and 1 got very
  slightly worse (0.00044 at tau=0.90, against a loss of 0.67961).
  Reporting that rather than rounding it away matters, because
  "provably no worse" is the kind of claim that gets quoted without
  the word "expected" in front of it. The right reading is that
  rearrangement costs nothing measurable and removes an incoherence
  that would otherwise reach a replenishment system.

  It is a post-processing step and not a fix to the cause. The cause is
  five fits that do not know the others exist; a joint or monotone-
  constrained estimator would be the real answer and is not built.

THE TWO SHORTCUTS ERR IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS, which is the finding:

  sum-of-daily-quantiles assumes every day goes wrong TOGETHER (perfect
  dependence). It is 1.75x the bootstrap at L=28, tau=0.95 -- ordering
  that much extra inventory to cover a coincidence that does not happen.

  iid-normal scaling assumes the days are INDEPENDENT and scales sigma by
  sqrt(L). It is 0.96x the bootstrap on the same row -- and it is the
  version most safety-stock formulas actually implement.

  The block bootstrap sits between them because it uses the dependence
  the errors actually have rather than assuming either extreme. Nothing
  about it is clever: resample CONTIGUOUS blocks and the dependence
  comes along for free, with no correlation structure to name and no
  copula to fit.

WHICH SHORTCUT IS WORSE DEPENDS ON THE AUTOCORRELATION, and here it is
measured rather than assumed: lag-1 rho = +0.0483, which is very nearly
ZERO. So on THIS panel the iid formula is close to right (0.956x) and the
sum-of-quantiles shortcut is the badly wrong one. That ordering is a
property of the data, not a general result, and stating it the other way
round would be the easy mistake: with strongly autocorrelated errors the
iid version is the dangerous one, because it under-covers in exactly the
clustered weeks that cause the stockout.

The value of the bootstrap is therefore NOT that it beat both shortcuts
by a lot here. It is that it did not need to be told which regime it was
in. A planner who picks the iid formula is making an assumption about
autocorrelation whether or not they know it, and this panel is the lucky
case where the assumption happens to hold.

THIS IS THE DATA-2 JOIN. DATA-2 sized safety stock from HISTORICAL mean
and sd and named forecast integration as its most valuable missing piece;
its negative-binomial experiment then failed for precisely this reason --
an iid marginal, however well specified, cannot produce the right
lead-time quantile. This is the distribution it needed.

THE GAP IS THE POINT, NOT A DEFECT TO CLOSE.

  A quantile is not a linear functional of a distribution:
  P95(A+B) != P95(A) + P95(B) unless A and B are perfectly dependent.
  Forcing the store P95 to equal the sum of its items' P95s would not
  make the forecast coherent, it would make it WRONG -- it asserts that
  every item in the store has its bad week simultaneously.

  Adding up is a property of REALISATIONS. Every sample path here is
  coherent by construction, so the coherent P95 at the total is
  automatically BELOW the sum of item P95s by exactly the
  diversification the residual correlation supports. The ratio column
  is that diversification, measured.

  Note the ratio SHRINKS toward the bottom of the hierarchy and grows at
  the top: the more series you pool, the more the independent part of
  their errors cancels. Aggregate safety stock sized by summing item
  safety stocks is over-provisioned by that ratio, and it is the
  argument for holding buffer centrally rather than at the leaf.
